## 2. What §508(c)(1)(A) actually is

Most new organizations that want recognition under **§501(c)(3)** must notify the IRS (typically Form 1023).

**§508(c)(1)(A)** is a **mandatory exception** from that *notice/application* requirement for:

- **churches**,  
- their **integrated auxiliaries**, and  
- **conventions or associations of churches**.

**Critical correction of a common myth:**

| Myth | Reality |
|------|--------|
| “508 is a different status than 501(c)(3)” | **False.** Qualifying churches are **501(c)(3)** organizations; 508(c)(1)(A) only removes the *need to apply* for recognition |
| “508 means IRS cannot touch us” | **False.** Private inurement, political campaign bans, UBIT, and examination risk still apply |
| “Any group that files magic paperwork is a church” | **False.** IRS uses multi-factor church characteristics; sincerity and operation matter |

Churches that meet **§501(c)(3)** requirements are generally treated as tax-exempt **by operation of law** and need not file Form 1023 to *be* exempt — though many still seek a **determination letter** for banks/donors.

Sources: IRC §508(c)(1)(A); IRS materials on churches and automatic exemption; secondary analyses (e.g. Church Law Center correcting “508 vs 501” marketing).

## 3. Church vs religious nonprofit

- **Church** (for tax purposes): multi-factor IRS analysis (creed, worship, ecclesiastical government, regular services, membership, literature, etc. — not a rigid checklist of all 14).  
- **Religious organization that is not a “church”:** may still be 501(c)(3) but **must** typically file Form 1023; loses some church-specific procedural advantages.

Option A “teaching order” must decide with counsel whether it will operate and be characterized as a **church**, an **integrated auxiliary**, or a **religious educational charity**.

## 4. Limits that still bind

1. **Private inurement / private benefit** — net earnings cannot inure to founders as owners.  
2. **No substantial political campaign intervention** (501(c)(3) restriction).  
3. **Unrelated business income** can be taxable.  
4. **Employment, payroll, state fundraising, zoning** still exist.  
5. **Fraud / sham** — manufactured belief for commercial evasion is not protected (*Ballard* sincerity doctrine; FTC/state UDAAP patterns).  
6. **Donors & banks** may still demand a determination letter even when Form 1023 is optional for churches.
